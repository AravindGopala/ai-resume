
I am an embedded software engineer with 10+ years of experience building and owning firmware platforms that run at fleet scale. Today I am the technical owner of a vehicle gateway platform deployed across several hundred thousand connected commercial vehicles, where I architected the multi-vendor GNSS and multi-modem cellular abstractions the fleet runs on, and I now also lead connectivity for a next-generation AI dashcam platform.

My work lives close to the hardware — C, C++, and Assembly on Embedded Linux and FreeRTOS, U-Boot and kernel bring-up, CAN/I2C/SPI, and LTE, GPS, and Wi-Fi integration across hardware revisions — but the problems I am usually handed are the ones that cross a boundary. A protocol defect that only reproduces at fleet scale. A cost figure nobody can attribute. An incident two other functions have formally exhausted. I close those by collecting evidence on the device when it cannot be seen from the backend, then shipping the fix as configuration so the fleet can be steered without a release.

I care as much about what happens after the fix. I have rebuilt CI and the local development environment for an entire embedded team, prototyped remote debugging into live fleet devices, carried a release train, and mentored engineers to deliver flagship work under my direction rather than delivering it myself. Instrument first, establish a baseline on real hardware, isolate the trigger, and only then change code — that is the method I apply, and the one I try to hand on.
